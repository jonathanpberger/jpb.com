Many Dvorak typists change their keyboard layout in software, but leave the physical keys in their Qwerty places. When I switched to Dvorak a few years ago, I found it difficult to use a Qwerty-emblazoned keyboard. Touch-typing was no problem, but keyboard shortcuts were problematic (especially in Adobe Creative Suite). Its tough to look at one key and mean another. For example, the “V” key is a very commonly used shortcut in most Adobe apps; in most apps, it restores the cursor to the default “Select” tool, so its used pretty much every time you’re finished using any other tool. On a Qwerty keyboard its bottom-row, left index finger. In Dvorak, that key is marked as “K”, while “V” is bottom-row, right ring-finger. Aggh! If I plant both hands on the keyboard I could find it, but that defeats the speed gain of using shortcut keys. Another approach is to switch to Qwerty, hit the key, and switch back to Dvorak, but that defeats the speed gain of using shortcut keys. Or I could learn a convoluted series of transpositions (“ummm…in Dvorak ‘K’, not ‘V’ means ‘Select tool’”), but that’s insane. So I decided to tear up my keyboard and move the physical keys. Which turns out to be surprisingly easy. Having done it quite a few times, I can now convert a keyboard in about 15 minutes. The first time I did it was on an old Macbook (instructable here), but more recent Apple keyboards use a slightly different mechanism (maybe I’ll get around to making another how-to).
